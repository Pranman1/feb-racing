\section*{The ask}

One 1:10-scale autonomous car on the RoboRacer (F1TENTH) standard, plus a cardboard lab
track: \textbf{\$1{,}865 recommended}, \$1{,}300 minimum, \$3{,}400 for the official
reference build. The FSAE car is on a two-year cycle and is not available for autonomous
work this season; this car is what the autonomous team develops, tests and learns on in the
meantime, and the pipeline built on it is the one that goes onto the FSAE car next year.

\subsection*{Sensor to actuator}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Sense.} Lidar driver publishes a scan; VESC driver publishes wheel odometry and IMU; camera driver publishes an image.
  \item \textbf{Perceive.} Walls or cones located relative to the car; cones classified by colour from the image and ranged by the lidar; later, position on a map.
  \item \textbf{Plan.} A target bearing and speed: the widest gap at first, a point on a raceline later.
  \item \textbf{Control.} Steering angle from the bearing, throttle from the speed error against odometry.
  \item \textbf{Actuate.} The VESC drives the servo and the motor, gated by the joystick dead-man. Every topic is recorded for replay.
\end{enumerate}
The nodes and topic names are the same as in the team's simulator, so a stack developed there is dropped onto the car with only the driver nodes swapped.

\subsection*{The lidar choice}

The lidar sets the speed the car can drive safely: at 10 to 12~Hz the car moves about 25~cm
between scans at 3~m/s, which is the practical limit for the cheaper units.

\section{Why it is worth it}

\subsection*{For the autonomous team this semester}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The FSAE car is being built and is not available for autonomous work; without a platform the team has nothing to run on until next year.
  \item Members stay for what they can see move. A lap time keeps someone a semester; a car that turns on their code keeps them for years.
  \item Real dynamics and real sensors cannot be simulated: grip that changes with the floor, servo backlash, battery sag, a lidar that sees chair legs and reflections.
  \item New members learn wiring, power, safety, motor control and ROS~2 on hardware at low cost and low risk.
  \item The simulator and the car share one codebase, so every member's stack has a place to go.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Carrying over to the FSAE car next year}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Same pipeline.} Cone detection with camera plus lidar, a midline between cones, a raceline, a tracking controller: the Formula Student Driverless stack, at small scale.
  \item \textbf{Same tools.} ROS~2 nodes and topics, sensor drivers, bag recording and replay, sim-to-real testing, safety procedures. These transfer directly.
  \item \textbf{Same method.} Develop in the simulator, transfer to the car, measure the gap, close it. The team arrives at the FSAE car already practised at this.
  \item \textbf{What does not transfer:} vehicle dynamics and tuned parameters. A 3~kg car says nothing about a 250~kg car's tyres, and the proposal does not claim it does.
\end{itemize}

\section{Will it work}

The build is the F1TENTH/RoboRacer platform, assembled by hundreds of university teams since
2016 from a public build guide, drawings and drivers; the parts list above is that guide's own
bill of materials with cheaper sensor and controller choices. Driving laps of a lab track is a
solved problem, and the team's simulator already runs the same car, sensors and software. The
risks are elsewhere: the car needs one named owner for assembly, batteries and access, or it
goes unused; integration takes two to four weekends before the first autonomous lap; laps come
quickly but mapping, racelines and perception are real work and will fill the semester;
crashes break bumpers and, occasionally, the lidar, which the spares line covers. With an
owner, the chance of the car never driving is low; the more likely outcome is a car that laps
within a month and a team spending the rest of the semester making it faster and smarter.

\section{Options set aside}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The team's Tamiya chassis.} A motorless touring-car kit. Saves about \$300 net for a semester of mechanical work with no drawings to copy, does not match the simulator's car, and is not eligible for any RoboRacer event. Kept as a bench mule.
  \item \textbf{A 1:4 or 1:5 car.} No 1:4 road car exists; the nearest are 1:5-class trucks (Traxxas X-Maxx, Arrma Kraton 8S, Losi 5IVE-T). Closer to FSAE in size and able to carry the team's RoboSense M1 lidar, but about \$3{,}500 even with the M1 and the Alameda test track counted, and a 10~kg car at 50~km/h cannot be run on campus: every test is an off-site session. A credible second step once the 1:10 programme is running, not a first car.
\end{itemize}
